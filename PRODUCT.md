# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Capman serves solo technical power users investigating, debugging, and reusing
their own work; non-technical professionals who need a clear record of their
methods; and teams consuming deliberately published organizational knowledge.
Personal activity is private by default. Team users must never see another
person's raw capture, private history, or unpublished deductions.

## Product Purpose

Capman captures cognitive workflows and turns successful work into searchable,
evidence-linked playbooks and knowledge. It helps people recover prior context,
replay effective methods, and selectively promote safe derived knowledge to a
team library.

## Brand Personality

Precise, evidence-led, selectively collaborative. The product should feel like
a reliable investigation instrument for individual work and a trustworthy,
privacy-aware library when knowledge is shared with a team.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic metric-card dashboards, decorative AI visual effects, noisy
always-on graphs, and collaboration models that blur private personal capture
with team-visible material. Do not make a technical investigation tool feel
like a social feed or a casual note-taking app.

## Design Principles

1. Evidence before assertion: every durable conclusion should expose where it
   came from and how confident Capman is.
2. Personal by default: raw personal activity remains private unless a user
   explicitly publishes a redacted, derived artifact.
3. One focused task at a time: navigation supports investigation and reuse,
   rather than presenting every capability as an equal destination.
4. Progressive disclosure: advanced maps, context assembly, and storage detail
   appear when useful, not as constant interface noise.
5. Selective collaboration: the Team Library is a distinct, auditable scope,
   not a filtered view of a person's private workspace.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG AA contrast requirements, support complete keyboard navigation and
visible focus states, and respect reduced-motion preferences. The UI must make
state understandable without relying solely on color or animation.
